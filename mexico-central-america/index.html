Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean

Official and primary records of old chemical weapons and former U.S. firing ranges in Panama, and mine disasters and mine pollution in Mexico, Guatemala, the Dominican Republic and Jamaica. Of the 7 records, 6 are verified, 0 were corrected against an official or primary source, and 1 is unverified and marked as such. 14 places in the region already recorded elsewhere on this site, including Puerto Rico, are listed as pointers.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. If you find something that may be a mine or unexploded ordnance, don’t touch it: mark the spot, move away the way you came, and tell the police or local authorities. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; Puerto Rico sites are on the U.S. pages and in alerts where they qualify.

Old chemical weapons and former firing ranges (2)

  • San José Island old chemical weapons (Pearl Islands) — Panamá, Panama Verified
    Republic of Panama; OPCW verification · chemical munitions (old chemical weapons)
    OPCW: Panama informed the Secretariat that all eight declared old chemical weapons had been destroyed; an OPCW inspection team observed the process. Panama (EC-86/NAT.20): OPCW-Panama agreement on inspections on San José Island dated 12 Jul 2017; OPCW technical verification team on the island 13-25 Sep 2017.
    Status: 8 declared old chemical weapons destroyed 2017
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: OPCW EC-86/DG.32, Director-General's opening statement to EC-86 (Oct 2017) · OPCW EC-86/NAT.20, Statement by Panama to the 86th Executive Council (10 Oct 2017)
  • Former US Canal Zone firing ranges (UXO) transferred to Panama — Panama Verified
    Former US military ranges (Canal Area) · unexploded ordnance
    US State Dept (Hidden Killers 2001): US military records indicate that since 1984 UXO killed seven Panamanians and one US soldier, all from trespassing into restricted range impact areas; after US clearance the US turned over 7,675 acres to Panama.
    Status: transferred 1999; UXO left in 'preserved land'
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: U.S. Department of State, 'Hidden Killers 2001: The World's Landmine Problem' (archived 2001-2009 site)

  • Buenavista del Cobre acidified copper-sulfate spill (Bacanuchi and Sonora rivers) — Sonora, Mexico Verified
    Copper mine leachate spill · Operator: Buenavista del Cobre, S.A. de C.V. and/or Minera México · 2014-08-06
    PROFEPA: spill of almost 40,000 m3 of acidified copper-sulfate leachate detected at 10:00 on 6 Aug 2014 entering the Bacanuchi river (Sonora river tributary); inspection act of 8 Aug 2014; criminal complaint filed with the PGR environmental unit for crimes under Art. 414 of the Federal Penal Code.
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: PROFEPA, 'Presenta PROFEPA denuncia penal contra responsables de derrame en río Bacanuchi' (Aug 2014)
  • Marlin gold mine, San Miguel Ixtahuacán / Sipacapa — San Marcos, Guatemala Verified
    Closed gold-silver mine · Operator: Glamis Gold, then Goldcorp (Oct 2006 - May 2017); Newmont since 2019 · -2017
    Newmont (2023): Marlin was owned and operated by Glamis Gold, then by Goldcorp from Oct 2006 until May 2017 when it ceased operating; Newmont bought Goldcorp in 2019 when Marlin was already in closure; physical reclamation completed late 2020; post-closure commitments continue.

  • Pueblo Viejo gold mine and El Naranjo tailings facility — Dominican Republic Unverified
    Open-pit gold mine; new tailings storage facility · Operator: Barrick (Pueblo Viejo Dominicana)
