About this site

A sourced tracker of facilities that store or destroy old or unstable energetics, published by D.A. Williams. It is not an official U.S. government document and should not be mistaken for one.

Status: Public
Built: 2026-11-23 (America/Phoenix calendar date)
Byline: D.A. Williams · https://awesomegoy.com

What is in this cut

Includes every facility on EPA's current OB/OD list as of the March 11, 2025 table (the same EPA page is also marked July 14, 2026), including Department of Defense, Department of Energy, NASA, contractor, and territorial entries, plus Camp Minden, Savanna Army Depot, and Lake City Army Ammunition Plant, which are not on that current list. JMC conventional demil hubs named in the Oct 1, 2025 Hrycak briefing are flagged when they map to these facilities.

Verification labels

verified: the listed facts were confirmed from official or other allowed primary sources. partly verified: official identity (and usually the EPA listing or a documented incident) is confirmed, but inventory, current operations, or some incident details remain incomplete. unverified: a claim was recorded but not confirmed against a primary source. Blank fields mean no source, not a zero or a no.

Most sites in this first cut are labeled partly verified: the EPA identity, address, permit listing, and FRS coordinates are confirmed, but this site has not yet built a full incident or inventory record. Sites with a deeper research note still carry gaps, which are stated on the site page.

Coordinates

Latitude and longitude are EPA FRS LATITUDE83/LONGITUDE83 for the site's RCRA ID when available. Collection method and accuracy are recorded when FRS provides them. Coordinates are not guessed or manually offset.

Sources

Preferred sources are EPA and state environmental records, GAO, DoD Inspector General, DTIC, Army and other official releases, court filings, AP, Reuters, and local news. This site does not cite NYT, CNN, MSNBC/MS NOW, Fox News, Snopes, or PolitiFact.

Primary starting sources:

  • EPA current OB/OD facilities list (table last updated March 11, 2025)
  • EPA Facility Registry Service, facility interests layer (LATITUDE83 / LONGITUDE83)
  • Orest Hrycak, acting Product Director for Demilitarization, Demil Enterprise Strategic Plan, Oct 1, 2025 (PAO# 794-25)
  • GAO-15-538, conventional ammunition awaiting demilitarization (2015)
  • Army FY2026 Procurement of Ammunition justification book, P-40 for line 4099EP1700 (June 2025), listed at Army budget materials

What is still left out

  • Facilities that are not on EPA's current OB/OD table and were not independently confirmed in this pass as currently operating RCRA OB/OD units. Historical or closed OB/OD units in RCRAInfo are omitted unless separately documented (the three extra sites are Camp Minden, Savanna Army Depot, and Lake City AAP).
  • EPA's Explosive Hazardous Wastes page states that 65 OB/OD facilities were still operating under interim status or a permit as of September 2024, from a cumulative 225 TSDFs that have had OB/OD units. The March 11, 2025 current-list table has 64 rows. This site did not independently identify the additional currently operating unit, if any, that accounts for that difference.
  • A RCRAInfo public API key was not obtained in this pass, and an ECHO hazardous-waste web-service query was rate-limited (HTTP 429), so no additional currently operating units were confirmed from those systems beyond the official EPA table.
  • Citations still exclude NYT, CNN, MSNBC/MS NOW, Fox News, Snopes, and PolitiFact.

ZIP search

The home-page “Is there one near me?” tool looks up a five-digit U.S. ZIP against a compact table of ZIP Code Tabulation Area internal centroids from the U.S. Census Bureau 2025 ZCTA gazetteer (file dated 2025; INTPTLAT and INTPTLONG, rounded to four decimal degrees). That file is a U.S. government work in the public domain. ZCTAs are generalized representations of ZIP Codes used for statistics; they are not identical to every USPS ZIP Code, and some postal ZIPs have no ZCTA centroid in this table.

Distances are straight-line (great-circle) miles from that ZCTA center, or from a location the browser reports after a “Use my location” click. They are not driving distance and not a measure of risk. The result list is the closest five sites in this inventory, plus any other dataset site within 100 miles. The ZIP table is loaded in the browser only when a ZIP is searched. No search or location data is sent to a server.

Map layers (schools and surface water)

Each site page can show a static overlay of public schools and surface water within about 10 miles of the EPA FRS coordinate. The extracts are built at compile time. The browser does not call a live API and no API key is used.

  • Public school points come from the National Center for Education Statistics EDGE Public School Locations, 2023-24 file (download). NCES states that the file is in the public domain. Private schools, child-care sites, and schools opened after that collection are not included. Guam, Hawaii, and the Northern Mariana Islands are in the NCES file when they were reported to the Common Core of Data.
  • Rivers, streams (stream order 3 and higher), and waterbodies of about 4 hectares or more in the conterminous United States come from the EPA Office of Water NHDPlus map service. For Hawaii, Guam, and the Northern Mariana Islands, which are outside NHDPlus coverage, this site uses the USGS National Hydrography Dataset small-scale territory layers from The National Map. Both are U.S. government works in the public domain. If a territory extract could not be retrieved, the site page says so and the school layer still appears.
  • Public water wells are not mapped. The public USGS NWIS site service returns mixed groundwater inventories (monitoring, domestic, and other wells) without a consistent public-supply use flag in the fields this site could retrieve, so well locations were omitted rather than shown without that distinction.

Distances in the overlay are straight-line miles from the site coordinate, not driving distance and not a measure of risk. A feature may sit on or just inside a military installation. Presence on the map is not a finding that a school or river is contaminated.

Prevailing wind

Each site page includes a simple wind rose built from a 2016-2025 frequency table at the nearest Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS/AWOS station. IEM documents this service at mywindrose.py. The station name and straight-line distance are labeled. Direction is the direction the wind blows from (meteorological convention). This is a multi-year pattern, not a smoke forecast, and wind at the munitions site can differ from the airport station. IEM data-use information is at the IEM disclaimer.

Records request kit and neighbor stories

The records kit builds a letter in the browser from a fill-in form. Nothing is sent or stored. Agency routing links are official FOIA or state public-records pages checked for this site on November 23, 2026 (America/Phoenix calendar date). Those addresses can change.

The Neighbor stories page accepts first-person accounts from adults who live, work, or went to school near a tracked site. A human reviewer reads every submission before anything is posted; nothing is published automatically. The moderation policy and the privacy notice describe what is collected and how review works.

Printable fact sheets

Each site has a compact, letter-size fact sheet intended to print on one page. It carries verification labels on incidents, sources with archived-copy links when a Wayback snapshot was found, and the canonical site URL. It is not an official government document.

Source archive

Where the Internet Archive Wayback Machine availability API already had a snapshot of a source URL, this site links an "archived copy" next to the live source. If no snapshot was found, a rate-limited Save Page Now request was attempted. This site does not rehost copyrighted documents. A missing archived-copy link means no snapshot was retrieved, not that the live source is unreliable.

Canada section

The Canada section lists 864 sites from data/canada_sites.json. Pins appear on the map only when the file includes official latitude and longitude. Approximate pins use official Government of Canada place-name coordinates and are labelled as approximate. This site does not geocode site names. 687 sites are mapped; 177 have no published coordinate and appear in the table only.

See the Canada page for the inventory, filters, and counts.

About “Closed” status: DND’s published “Closed” label does not mean a site is clear of unexploded ordnance. The DND UXO Legacy Sites Program page states that sites with no or low UXO risk may be administratively closed, that administratively closed sites can be re-opened at any time if new information suggests an unacceptable UXO risk, and that no technology available today guarantees all UXO on a site have been located and removed.

Europe section

The Europe page lists 9,737 official EMODnet Human Activities dumped-munitions records (9,459 points and 278 areas). 1,054 records are chemical or mixed chemical and conventional. 8,322 of the point records cite OSPAR ODIMS as the information provider; those are encounter reports, not designated dump sites. Country is derived from Marine Regions EEZ v12 (2023) and is blank for 214 records in high seas or shared waters. Official polygon outlines are kept on 255 of the 278 area records when the ring was reasonably small; the others are shown as centroids and labelled as area centroids. Scotland’s NMPi historic munitions disposal sites layer is not included, because it is not downloadable.

EMODnet Human Activities, Dumped Munitions (CETMAR / EMODnet), CC BY 4.0. Official record: EMODnet metadata · Human Activities portal.

Made with Marine Regions, Flanders Marine Institute (VLIZ), EEZ v12 2023, CC BY 4.0. marineregions.org. Country is derived from those exclusive economic zone polygons and is not an EMODnet attribute. Joint regimes, overlapping claims, and high seas are labelled High seas / shared waters. This page uses a processed compact copy of the official features (polygon centroids, country assignment, and a smaller file for the map and table) and is not endorsed by EMODnet, CETMAR, or VLIZ.

Downloads of the processed page copy: europe-compact.json · europe-sites.csv · europe-geoms.json. The official EMODnet dataset remains the source of record, via the metadata record.

OB/OD permits and air records

Each facility page has an “EPA permit and air records” section, checked on Nov 25, 2026. All 64 facilities on EPA’s current open burning / open detonation list (updated March 11, 2025) are matched: 60 permitted and 4 under interim status. 51 have a permit PDF posted on EPA’s Subpart X page (EPA notes posted permits may be expired or modified). Facility registry IDs and hazardous-waste roles come from EPA’s Facility Registry Service (FRS). Compliance fields come from EPA ECHO, and air-program fields from EPA ICIS-AIR downloads (files dated Nov 22, 2026; 44 facilities have an air-program record).

The air and compliance data are whole-plant data, not proof the open burning caused a violation. They describe the whole facility, not the open burning or detonation unit alone. Map points are the existing EPA FRS points; none were moved. Hawthorne Main Base and the New Bomb Facility share one FRS registry and point, so they plot at the same spot. Savanna Army Depot, Camp Minden and Lake City Army Ammunition Plant are not on EPA’s current list. Download: obod.json.

U.S. sea-disposal sites

The sea dumps page lists the 72 sites in DoD’s FY2009 Defense Environmental Restoration Program report to Congress, Sea Disposal of Military Munitions (figures 10-6 to 10-77), with supporting detail from the FY2007 report, Appendix S and the November 2016 DoD Report to Congress. Each site’s name, distance from shore (nautical miles), depth (feet), items dumped and chemical agent totals were checked against the official tables on Nov 25, 2026. 23 sites include chemical weapons. The coast shown comes from DoD’s site code (66 sites; blank where DoD did not name one).

Only HI-06 Ordnance Reef is mapped, at the approximate middle of the “Unexploded Ordnance” area on NOAA chart 19357 (NOAA MarineCadastre munitions layer; also surveyed in NOAA’s 2007 Ordnance Reef report). General charted dumping areas that are not labeled with a DoD site code are not plotted. Email alerts use 50 miles for Ordnance Reef and the coastal state for the other sites where DoD names one. Download: seadump.json.

DOE Legacy Management sites and Navajo abandoned uranium mines

The nuclear legacy page lists all 108 sites in DOE Office of Legacy Management’s official GEMS “LM Sites (All)” layer (updated Nov 24, 2026; 73 DOE sites and 35 FUSRAP sites already transferred to DOE), matched to DOE’s LM site list. Every site has an official GEMS point. The Plowshare/Vela Uniform Records, CO, Site is a records site in Grand Junction, not a contamination site, and is left out of distances and alerts. The 20 active USACE FUSRAP sites are unchanged.

The 523 abandoned uranium mines come from EPA Region 9’s Navajo Nation AUM Site Screening map service, matching EPA’s published count. EPA’s inventory covers mines on or within one mile of the Navajo Nation; 19 mine points sit just outside the boundary. The state shown for a mine is computed from its point. Program and health information: EPA’s Navajo abandoned uranium mines program, EPA health guidance and the Navajo Nation EPA. Both layers were checked field by field on Nov 25, 2026. They appear in the ZIP search, on state pages and in facility nearby lists, and email alerts use 50 miles. Download: nuclear.json.

Formerly Used Defense Sites (FUDS)

The FUDS page shows all 10,150 properties and all 1,685 munitions response site polygons from the USACE FUDS Data map service (FY24; property points, project points and MRS polygons), checked field by field on Nov 25, 2026. 1,493 properties have a munitions response (MMRP) project and are labeled “FUDS munitions”; the other 8,657 are labeled “Other FUDS” (1,385 with hazardous waste, containerized waste or building demolition projects, 7,272 with no project). 8,847 have an official point; 1,303 are list-only, including 15 whose official coordinates were 0, 0. Two are listed with a caution and not mapped because the official point falls in a different state (Finley AFS Z-29, listed in North Dakota; Atlas S-3, listed in Vermont). Duplicate Palau property numbers were given unique IDs.

Each MRS uses the official feature name, with the parent property’s DoD FUDS name and county. MRSPP values come from the official project points; MRSPP priorities run from 1 (highest potential hazard) to 8 (lowest). USACE publishes no code list for 09, 10 and 11; they are shown as DoD’s three alternative ratings (evaluation pending, no longer required, no known or suspected hazard) in that order, which matches project status in the data. Seventeen large ranges have boundaries more than 50 km (31 miles) from the property point; that is how the official data looks, not an error. Polygons are simplified (about 0.0008 degree tolerance, rounded to 4 decimals). Property details and boundaries load one state at a time. ZIP search distances for munitions properties use the nearest mapped boundary (or “inside”) where one exists, otherwise the property point. Email alerts cover every mapped FUDS property within 50 miles.

PFAS, Superfund and FUSRAP layers

The ZIP search also shows nearby Superfund, FUSRAP and military PFAS sites. These layers come straight from government data and are kept separate from the munitions inventory above.

  • Superfund: EPA’s Superfund National Priorities List (NPL) sites with status information, data as of September 30, 2026. 1,840 sites: 1,337 on the list, 37 proposed and 466 deleted. The federal facility label (177 sites) comes from EPA’s SEMS federal facility list. Each site links to its official EPA profile. What is Superfund?
  • Military PFAS: the Department of Defense PFAS status chart (DoD PFAS program), data as of June 30, 2026. 727 installations that DoD is assessing for PFAS use or possible release. 212 have an official map point checked Nov 25, 2026 (DoD MIRTA, EPA SEMS/FRS or USACE FUDS points) and appear on maps and in ZIP distances; the rest are listed by state on the PFAS page. Nearby town water results come from EPA UCMR 5 (file dated 2026-10-15): 172 mapped bases have a public water system within 5 miles and 67 have a PFOA or PFOS detection there. These are town water systems within 5 miles, not tests on the base.
  • FUSRAP: the 20 active U.S. Army Corps of Engineers FUSRAP sites on the DOE Office of Legacy Management list, checked against USACE, DOE LM and EPA records on Nov 25, 2026. 17 have a checked map point. 3 (Joslyn Manufacturing and Supply Company, DuPont Chambers Works, Staten Island Warehouse) are marked “location not confirmed” and are listed on the FUSRAP page but left off the map and out of ZIP distances. Download: fusrap.json.

Distances are straight-line miles and are not a measure of risk. Downloads: superfund.json · pfas.json. Other maps, including advocacy group maps, are on More maps.
