Middle East

Official and primary records of 14 current and former U.S. military sites, 16 nuclear sites and IAEA-reported locations, 24 explosion, landmine, unexploded ordnance (UXO) and depleted uranium records, and 12 uranium, phosphate and metal mines across the Gulf states, Iraq, Iran, the Levant, Yemen and Türkiye. Of the 66 records, 52 are verified, 1 was corrected against an official source, and 13 are unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. If you find something that may be a mine or unexploded ordnance, don’t touch it: mark the spot, move away the way you came, and tell the police or local authorities. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; Middle East records are not part of alerts.

Read before using the map

  • Shaded areas are not hazard boundaries. Each of the 9 shaded areas is a whole country or province outline (GADM 4.1) standing in for reported minefield or UXO zones inside it. No national authority publishes open minefield outlines.
  • Israel’s shading uses a placeholder boundary file. The GADM 4.1 outline for Israel includes the Golan Heights. It is used only to place the record and is not a statement on sovereignty or borders.
  • 13 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Camp Arifjan (Kuwait), Camp Buehring (Kuwait), Al Tanf Garrison (Syria), Dimona (Israel), Soreq (Israel), Al-Tuwaitha / Osirak (Iraq), Barakah (UAE), Port of Beirut explosion, 2020, Mahd adh Dhahab mine (Saudi Arabia), Al Jalamid mine (Saudi Arabia), Al Amar mine (Saudi Arabia), Ruseifa mines (Jordan), Akashat mine (Iraq). They are kept for context because a key fact could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • Strikes in 2025 and 2026 are described only as the cited official page states them. Operation names appear only where a U.S. government page uses them, and are attributed. Statements that could be read only through a search summary, or whose page could not be opened, are marked “Reported”. Where the IAEA does not say who carried out a 2026 attack, neither does this page.
  • Camp Arifjan’s point is flagged. It comes from a 1980–1997 weather-station record that predates the U.S. camp, so it may not mark the camp.
  • Military points are airfield-level, taken from NOAA weather-station records, not base outlines. Incirlik is in Türkiye (a NATO ally under U.S. European Command) and is included for regional context.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. 34 records have no usable official coordinate and are listed below but not mapped, including every Iranian and Israeli nuclear site (IAEA reports give site names, not coordinates). Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

23 points and 9 shaded stand-in areas are mapped. Unverified records have a dashed yellow outline. The five depleted uranium points in Kuwait are IAEA sampling points, not site centres.
