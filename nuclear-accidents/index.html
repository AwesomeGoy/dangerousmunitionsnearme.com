Nuclear accidents and waste

Nuclear and radiological accidents, nuclear-waste repositories and legacy contamination worldwide, from regulators, the IAEA and national agencies. 37 records: 31 verified, 2 corrected against a better official source and 4 unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. It is a selection of documented places, not a complete list. ZIP alerts on this site do not include these records.

Read before using this page

  • 4 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Sequoyah Fuels UF6 cylinder rupture, Mayapuri cobalt-60 scrap-market accident, Mayak legacy waste: Techa River discharges and Lake Karachay (reservoir V-9), West Valley Demonstration Project (former commercial reprocessing plant). Figures in them are reported, not confirmed in an official source.
  • INES levels are given only where an official source states them. Casualty figures follow the cited official source; where official sources disagree, the conflict is noted.
  • Points come only from official sources: EPA facility registry and Superfund points, the Office for Nuclear Regulation’s published site grid references, and the Chornobyl plant operator’s published site point. A point marks a whole site, not a single reactor, tank or store. All other records are list only.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

14 records are mapped as points. Unverified records have a dashed yellow outline. The other 23 records have no usable official coordinate and are listed below only.

  • Chornobyl (Chernobyl) Nuclear Power Plant Unit 4 accident — Chornobyl NPP, Pripyat, Kyiv Oblast, Kiev, UKR Verified
    Date: 1986-04-26
    Event: reactor explosion and graphite fire (RBMK-1000)
    INES level: 7
    Casualties: 28 workers died of acute radiation effects within four months; 2 died on site of non-radiological causes; 106 ARS cases (NRC)
    Status now: Exclusion zone in force; NSC damaged by the Feb 2025 drone strike and fire, repairs coordinated by EBRD.
    Power surge during a reactor systems test destroyed Unit 4 on 26 Apr 1986; the explosion and following fire released massive amounts of radioactive material (NRC). 28 of the site's ~600 workers died of severe radiation effects in the first four months and 106 more had acute radiation sickness; ~115,000 people were evacuated in 1986 and ~220,000 later; 30 km exclusion zone (NRC, citing UNSCEAR 2008). Turbogenerator trip signal blocked at 01:23:04 (INSAG-7). Rated INES 7 (BABS). New Safe Confinement completed and handed over in 2019; struck by a drone on 14 Feb 2025 (EBRD), after which the IAEA found the NSC cannot currently perform its two main safety functions (GOV/2026/7).
    Point is the plant operator’s published point for the Chornobyl NPP industrial site, not Unit 4 itself. Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: EBRD, 'Making Chornobyl safe' (New Safe Confinement; February 2025 drone strike) · IAEA Board of Governors, GOV/2026/7 'Nuclear Safety, Security and Safeguards in Ukraine' · IAEA, INSAG-7 'The Chernobyl Accident: Updating of INSAG-1' (Safety Series No. 75-INSAG-7, 1992) · Swiss Federal Office for Civil Protection (BABS), Gefährdungsdossier 'KKW-Unfall' (INES classifications of Chernobyl, Fukushima, Lucens) · U.S. NRC, Backgrounder on Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant Accident (page could not be opened; read in a search summary) · SSE Chornobyl NPP, Contact information (industrial-site coordinates)

  • Kyshtym accident: waste-tank explosion at the Mayak Production Association — Mayak PA near Ozyorsk (Kyshtym), Chelyabinsk Oblast, Chelyabinsk, RUS Verified
    Date: 1957-09-29
    Event: chemical explosion of a high-level liquid-waste storage tank
    INES level: 6
    Casualties: No deaths from the explosion (Mayak PA)
    Status now: EURT partly a state nature reserve; Mayak operating (not re-verified).
    Cooling failure in a concrete tank of highly active nitrate-acetate waste led to a chemical explosion that released fission products over Chelyabinsk, Sverdlovsk and Tyumen provinces; about 2 million Ci dispersed off the site; the 0.1 Ci/km2 Sr-90 trace reached 300 km (INFCIRC/368). About 600 people were evacuated within 7-10 days and about 10,000 later (INFCIRC/368). Tank 14 destroyed, no deaths; ~20 MCi total, 90% fell on the production site; contaminated area named the East Urals Radioactive Trace (EURT); later ranked INES 6 (Mayak PA, 2013).
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.

    Casualties: None reported
