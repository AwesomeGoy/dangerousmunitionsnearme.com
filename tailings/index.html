Coal-ash and tailings dam failures

Failures of coal-ash, coal-refuse and mine-tailings dams and impoundments, with release volumes and casualties from official investigations. 12 records: 9 verified, 2 corrected against a better official source and 1 unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. It is a selection of documented places, not a complete list. ZIP alerts on this site do not include these records.

Coal ash and coal refuse (5; 2 mapped)

Coal-ash ponds and coal-refuse impoundments that failed.

  • TVA Kingston Fossil Plant coal-ash dike failure — Harriman / Kingston, Roane County (714 Swan Pond Road), Tennessee, USA Verified
    Date: 2008-12-22
    Material: coal fly ash sludge
    Casualties: No deaths reported in the EPA pages read; three homes destroyed and a nearby neighborhood evacuated
    Volume released: about 5.4 million cubic yards of fly ash (EPA)
    Status now: Removal under an enforceable EPA-TVA agreement signed 11 May 2009 (CERCLA, EPA oversight). The EPA archive pages are no longer updated; current completion status not confirmed from an official page read.
    The northeastern dike of an ash dewatering/disposal cell (one of three settling cells, permitted by TDEC as a Class II solid-waste landfill) failed, releasing about 5.4 million cubic yards of fly ash into the Emory and Clinch Rivers and over about 300 acres outside the ash storage area. The wave destroyed three homes, ruptured a natural-gas line, disrupted power and covered a railway and roads. EPA sampling found arsenic, cobalt, iron and thallium in ash-contaminated residential soil above residential Superfund soil-screening values; treated drinking water from the Kingston plant met federal standards. TCLP testing showed the ash would not be classed as hazardous waste.

  • Duke Energy Dan River Steam Station coal-ash release — Eden, Rockingham County (South Edgewood Road), North Carolina, USA Verified
    Date: 2014-02-02
    Material: coal ash and ash-pond wastewater
    Casualties: None reported
    Volume released: 39,000 tons of coal ash and 27 million gallons of ash-pond water (EPA estimate); DOJ factual statement: 30,000-39,000 tons and about 27 million gallons
    Status now: Release stopped; settlement removal complete (July 2014). Natural-resource damage assessment by NC, VA and USFWS trustees.
    A corroded five-foot elbow joint in the 60-year-old corrugated-metal section of a 48-inch stormwater pipe under the primary ash basin buckled and failed, forming a sinkhole; ash and ash-pond water drained into the Dan River. USFWS reported ash travelling more than 62 miles downriver to the John H. Kerr Reservoir on the NC-VA line. A second (36-inch) pipe under the basin was found leaking and plugged on 21 Feb 2014. Under a 22 May 2014 EPA AOC, about 4,000 cubic yards of ash were removed from Schoolfield Dam, Town Creek sand bar and the Danville and South Boston water-treatment facilities (completed July 2014). DOJ's 2015 joint factual statement (filed with Duke Energy's plea agreement) records negligent Clean Water Act violations.
    Point is the EPA facility registry point for the facility (facility-level). Checked 2026-11-25.

  • Buffalo Creek coal-refuse dam failure (Pittston / Buffalo Mining Co. Dam No. 3) — Middle Fork of Buffalo Creek, Logan County (Saunders to Man), West Virginia, USA Verified
    Date: 1972-02-26
    Material: coal-preparation refuse, sludge and water
    Casualties: 125 killed (MSHA; Governor's commission: at least 118 dead plus 7 missing); about 1,000 injured (commission; MSHA says 1,100); 4,000 left homeless
    Volume released: 17.6 million cubic feet (132 million gallons) of water (Governor's Ad Hoc Commission)
    Status now: Historic disaster. No official coordinate for the dam found.
    Mine-waste Dam No. 3 of the Buffalo Mining Co. (a Pittston Co. division) on the Middle Fork failed just before 8 a.m. after heavy rain; the flood washed out two further refuse impoundments and travelled 17 miles to Man in about three hours, wiping out Saunders and partly or totally destroying 16 more communities. The commission found piping and sludge displacement in a dam built on weak slurry without an adequate spillway; MSHA describes the dams as end-dumped loose coarse refuse not built to then-current engineering standards.

  • Martin County Coal Corp. Big Branch refuse impoundment breakthrough — Inez, Martin County (Wolf Creek and Coldwater Fork), Kentucky, USA Corrected
    Date: 2000-10-11
    Material: coal slurry (fine coal refuse and water)
    Casualties: None (MSHA)
    Volume released: about 306 million gallons of slurry flowed into the 1-C Mine (MSHA); 250 million gallons in the National Response Center report
    Status now: Historic event; no official current-status page read.
    Slurry broke through the bottom of the 68-acre Big Branch Refuse Impoundment into the underground workings of the company's 1-C Mine, breached two sealed areas and exited at the South Mains and No. 2 North portals, flooding and blackening Wolf Creek and Coldwater Fork. The pool dropped an average of 14 ft (22% of the slurry above the mine). MSHA found the company had not followed its approved 1994/1995 impoundment sealing plan; internal erosion (piping) through a shot-rock 'seepage barrier' caused the breakthrough.
    Corrected: The widely repeated 250 million gallons (National Response Center report) is superseded by MSHA’s investigated estimate of 306 million gallons; both are shown.

  • Aberfan colliery spoil-tip collapse (Merthyr Vale Colliery) — Aberfan, Merthyr Vale Colliery, Wales, GBR Verified
    Date: 1966-10-21
    Material: colliery spoil (coal waste)
    Casualties: 144 killed, 116 of them children (Glamorgan Archives)
    Status now: Historic disaster; tribunal report held at The National Archives and Glamorgan Archives.
    A tip of coal waste from Merthyr Vale Colliery slid onto the village of Aberfan. The tribunal appointed by the Secretary of State for Wales on 26 Oct 1966 sat for 76 days and concluded that 'Blame for the disaster rests upon the National Coal Board'; on its recommendation, information on all NCB tips was assembled for the first time.

Mine tailings and mine waste (7; 2 mapped)

Tailings dams, waste dumps and mine-water releases.

  • Gold King Mine acid mine-water release (EPA excavation) — Cement Creek above Silverton, San Juan County (Bonita Peak Mining District), Colorado, USA Corrected
    Date: 2015-08-05
    Material: acid mine drainage and mine-waste sediment (heavy metals)
    Casualties: None (no workers harmed)
    Volume released: about 3 million gallons (USGS stream-gauge provisional 3,043,067 gallons); about 10,000 cubic yards of waste-rock dump washed out
    Status now: Within the Bonita Peak Mining District NPL site (listed 9 Sep 2016; Superfund id CON000802497).
    While EPA crews excavated above the collapsed Gold King adit during a removal assessment, pressurized water broke through the blockage and released acid mine water into Cement Creek, a tributary of the Animas River. The plume crossed three states and three tribal lands (Southern Ute, Navajo Nation, Ute Mountain Ute) over about 550 km (342 mi) to Lake Powell in about nine days. Not a tailings-dam failure in the strict sense: it was a mine-pool blowout through a collapsed portal, included here as a mine-waste release.
    Corrected: EPA’s initial estimate of about 1 million gallons (based on the size of the adit) was superseded by the USGS stream-gauge figure of 3,043,067 gallons (EPA). The map uses the EPA facility registry release point rather than the district-level Bonita Peak Superfund point.
    Related: Bonita Peak Mining District (Superfund page).
    Point is the EPA facility registry point for the facility (facility-level). Checked 2026-11-25.

  • Mount Polley Mine tailings storage facility breach — about 57 km northeast of Williams Lake, Cariboo Mining Division (Hazeltine Creek, Polley and Quesnel lakes), British Columbia, CAN Verified
    Date: 2014-08-04
    Material: copper-gold mine tailings and process water
    Casualties: None reported
    Volume released: between 21 and 25 million cubic metres of water and tailings, including about 10 million m3 of process water (BC Chief Inspector of Mines)
    Status now: Mine status 'Producer' in MINFILE (last edit 31 May 2024).
    The perimeter embankment of the tailings storage facility breached at about 1:08 a.m. (Chief Inspector) while 1 m short of its permitted crest. The BC-appointed Independent Expert Engineering Investigation and Review Panel (30 Jan 2015) found undrained failure in a glaciolacustrine foundation layer (Upper GLU) not detected by pre-failure site investigation. Discharge scoured Hazeltine Creek and entered Polley and Quesnel lakes. Operations were suspended on 4 Aug 2014 and resumed on 5 Aug 2015 (MINFILE).

  • Aznalcóllar (Boliden-Apirsa) tailings-pond wall failure — Aznalcóllar, Sevilla province (Agrio and Guadiamar rivers), Andalucía, ESP Verified
    Date: 1998-04-25
    Material: pyritic sludge and acid water
    Casualties: None reported in the source read
    Volume released: more than 6 million cubic metres of pyritic sludge and acid water (Junta de Andalucía)
    Status now: Restored as the Paisaje Protegido Corredor Verde del Guadiamar.
    The containment wall of the tailings pond at the Aznalcóllar mines failed in the early hours, releasing sludge and acid water down the Agrio and Guadiamar. Some 62 km of riverbank and 4,634 ha in nine Sevilla municipalities were affected; sludge settled over the first 40 km (over 3 m thick near the pond) and acid water reached Entremuros at the gates of Doñana National Park, where almost 1,800 ha of marsh were restored. The affected land became the Corredor Verde del Guadiamar (protected landscape, 2003).

  • Aurul S.A. Baia Mare cyanide tailings-dam overflow — Baia Mare (Lapuș-Someș-Tisza-Danube system), Maramureș, ROU Verified
    Date: 2000-01-30
    Material: cyanide-bearing gold-retreatment tailings water
    Casualties: None reported
    Volume released: about 100,000 cubic metres of tailings water with free cyanide and cyanide complexes (UNEP/OCHA)
    Status now: Historic event; current site status not confirmed from an official source read.
    High water in the Aurul pond overtopped and washed away about 25 m of dam, 2.5 m deep; the contaminant passed via the Lapuș and Someș into the Tisza (Hungary), the Danube (FR Yugoslavia) and on to the Black Sea. Aurul was retreating old tailings for gold and silver. The Maramureș prefect convened the county disaster commission on 31 Jan; the UNEP/OCHA mission (23 Feb-6 Mar 2000) was requested by Hungary, Romania and FR Yugoslavia.

  • MAL Ajka alumina plant red-mud Reservoir 10 failure (Kolontár) — Reservoir 10, Torna Creek valley between Ajka and Kolontár (Kolontár, Devecser, Somlóvásárhely), HUN Verified
    Date: 2010-10-04
    Material: alkaline bauxite residue (red mud) and liquor
    Casualties: 10 killed, 286 injured (ARIA/Hungarian authorities)
    Volume released: about 1,000,000 m3 of red sludge and water
    Status now: Historic event; current site status not confirmed from an official source read.
    At about 12:30 the western dyke of Reservoir 10 (4.5 million m3 capacity, in use since 1998, 21-25 m walls) broke over about 50 m; strongly alkaline red sludge flooded Kolontár, Devecser and Somlóvásárhely, about 1,000 ha of land were contaminated and over 10 million m3 of water polluted. Authorities worked to hold the mud in the Marcal river bed and keep the plume from reaching the Danube via the Rába and Mosoni-Duna; ARIA tags the event 'transboundary effects'. The Hungarian parliamentary inquiry found excess alkaline liquid above permitted levels to be the principal operational cause, with design, permitting and monitoring failures.
