Antarctica

Official and primary records of 26 current and former military-built or military-run stations and logistics sites, 7 nuclear reactor and radioisotope generator (RTG) sites, 6 contamination, waste and explosives sites, and 8 records on Antarctic minerals and the ban on mining. Of the 47 records, 42 are verified, 3 were corrected against a newer official source, and 2 are unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. Antarctica has no permanent residents; visits are permitted under the Antarctic Treaty system. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; Antarctica records are not part of alerts.

Read before using the map

  • 2 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Ellsworth Station, SNAP-7C generator, Minna Bluff. They are kept for context because a key fact could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • Little America I–V are list only. Their official positions now plot at sea, because the Ross Ice Shelf front they stood on has calved away. Ellsworth Station, also on floating ice, is list only too.
  • Some points are station-level. The Casey explosives store and the Mirny and Novolazarevskaya generator sites have no published location, so they are mapped at the station.
  • Four sites sit just off the simplified coastline: Marble Point (255 m), San Martín (242 m), O’Higgins (170 m) and Hallett (33 m) are on small islands or spits the coastline data doesn’t show. Each record says so.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. 16 records have no usable official coordinate, including all 8 minerals records, and are listed below but not mapped. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

31 records are mapped. Map view: a south polar stereographic projection (EPSG:3031), looking down on the South Pole, so stations are not stretched the way they are on ordinary web maps. The Prime Meridian (0°) points up. The coastline is the SCAR Antarctic Digital Database (v7.13, medium resolution) simplified to about 4 km; dark grey is land and light grey is floating ice shelf. There are no street-map tiles. Wilkes and Hallett have both a station record and a waste record at the same point; McMurdo, PM-3A and Winter Quarters Bay are close together, so zoom in.

Military and government stations (26; 21 mapped)

Sources include COMNAP, NSF, Antarctic Treaty records and the national programs. The Antarctic Treaty bans military measures such as bases and weapons testing, but allows military personnel and equipment for science and peaceful purposes.

Minerals: no mining, by treaty (8; all list only)

Mining is banned in Antarctica. Article 7 of the Protocol on Environmental Protection to the Antarctic Treaty (the Madrid Protocol, in force since 14 January 1998) prohibits any activity relating to mineral resources other than scientific research. The ban has no expiry date: until 2048 it can only be changed with the agreement of all Consultative Parties, and after 2048 a party can ask for a review conference, under strict voting rules. An earlier treaty that would have regulated mining (CRAMRA, 1988) never came into force. No official source reports any deposit being mined, and the U.S. Geological Survey found in 1983 that “no known petroleum or mineral resources occur in Antarctica.” These records cover the legal status and the best-known surveyed formations; none has a deposit coordinate, so all are list only. Reported large oil estimates have no official confirmation and are not included.
