Known energetics or munitions types

M6 propellant and clean-burning igniter (CBI) left from Explo Systems contractor storage of Army-origin material. These are HD 1.3 propellant/igniter materials, not primary explosives such as lead azide. A Louisiana National Guard release dated April 21, 2017, said the State of Louisiana contracted Explosive Service International on June 17, 2015, to destroy approximately 15,687,247 pounds of M6 and approximately 320,890 pounds of CBI, and that destruction of the M6 and CBI had been successfully completed in accordance with the contract. The Minden Press-Herald reported on October 24, 2018, that 15 pounds of M6 remained in a bunker thought too unstable to move. This site did not find an official 2019–2026 confirmation that those 15 pounds were later destroyed.

Current status

The main contained-burn disposal campaign was declared complete by the Louisiana National Guard on April 21, 2017; ESI was then to conduct about 30 days of cleanup, including burning residual debris, after which the chamber would not be used in that capacity again at Camp Minden. As of an October 24, 2018, Minden Press-Herald account of a Camp Minden Citizens Advisory Group meeting, 15 pounds of M6 were still reported in a bunker, the burn chamber was no longer in use and was up for sale, and Louisiana Military Department project coordinator Winston Matejowsky was quoted saying closure could not go on until that M6 was burned. This site has not independently confirmed, from an official 2019–2026 source, whether those 15 pounds remain, were burned, or were otherwise disposed of.
