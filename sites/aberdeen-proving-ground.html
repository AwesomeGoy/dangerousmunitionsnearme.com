Known energetics or munitions types

A May 20, 2022 Maryland Department of the Environment tentative-determination fact sheet and draft Controlled Hazardous Substance permit A-190 (EPA ID MD3210021355) describe these treatment units: the J-Field Open Detonation unit, for waste conventional explosives and munitions (and items fully decontaminated or formally found not to contain lethal agents); and Old Bombing Field open-burn and open-detonation units, used to treat waste conventional explosives and propellants, with open burning of waste propellants on steel pans and open detonation in a designated 18.4-acre site. The draft also lists other Edgewood Area hazardous-waste units operated by DEVCOM Chemical Biological Center and related organizations (storage, thermal treatment, Explosive Destruction System, Munitions Assessment and Processing System). Those descriptions are from the 2022 draft renewal, not from a retrieved final signed 2022–2026 permit. Separately, a 2024 emergency permit addressed about 35 pounds of potentially unstable TMETN at an Army Research Laboratory bunker.

Current status

MDE received a renewal application for CHS permit A-190 on August 14, 2019, which is before the February 15, 2020 expiration of the prior term. On May 20, 2022, MDE issued a tentative determination to renew the permit, with a draft 10-year permit. Maryland open-data records reviewed in this pass still listed permit A-190 as expired (term February 15, 2010–February 15, 2020) and not administratively extended. EPA's current OB/OD list, last updated March 11, 2025, still shows the facility as permitted with the February 15, 2020 expiration. This draft did not retrieve a final post-2022 signed CHS permit. Because the renewal application was received before expiration, the 40 CFR 270.51 continuation rule may apply, but a final issuance was not confirmed here.

2024-11-21verifiedMDE temporary emergency permit E2024-1 (oral approval November 21, 2024; written permit November 22, 2024; expired February 19, 2025) authorized on-site contained chemical treatment of approximately 35 pounds of trimethylolethane trinitrate (TMETN, CAS 3032-55-1) in a single container at Army Research Laboratory Bunker 1174 on Spesutie Island. Army explosives-safety experts were concerned the material may have become unstable after about 10 years of storage and might detonate if shipped. ARL proposed chemical neutralization by MuniRem Environmental, LLC, as an alternative to open detonation. The permit states there was no other TMETN on the inventory list.https://mde.maryland.gov/programs/land/Documents/APG%20Emergency%20Permit%202024/APG%20-%20emergency%20permit%2011-21-24%20signed.pdf

Notes

The 2024 TMETN action is a small-quantity instability case treated by proposed chemical neutralization under an emergency permit, not a bulk-drum detonation. Whether the work was completed, and whether CHS permit A-190 was finally renewed, were not independently confirmed.
