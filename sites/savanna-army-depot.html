EPA FRS RCRAINFO IL3210020803 (NAD83). GDT-ADDRESS MATCHING (GEOCODING); reference: entrance point of a facility or station. FRS also lists a SEMS point at 42.21, -90.313331; this draft uses the RCRA record and does not average the two.

Known energetics or munitions types

William Shaneyfelt, safety officer at Savanna Army Depot Activity, described Vietnam-era surplus bulk lead azide in deteriorating 55-gallon drums in a 1984 paper to the DoD Explosives Safety Board. Electrolysis destroyed about half the stockpile; the remainder was repacked. The public record reviewed for this draft does not establish what happened to the remaining drums after 1984.

Current status

A former Army depot. EPA FRS lists BRAC and SEMS interests under ID IL3210020803. It is not on EPA's current OB/OD operating list. Present land use and whether any energetic material remains were not independently confirmed in this draft.

Notable incidents

DateWhat is documentedSources
1984Shaneyfelt paper (21st Explosives Safety Seminar, Houston, Aug 28–30, 1984): surplus bulk lead azide in deteriorating drums; sale attempts failed; electrolytic destruction halted after about half the stockpile; remaining leaking drums were repacked. The paper also describes a process-building contamination accident in which sodium azide washdown reacted with brass/copper debris and caused a 'loud, though not extremely dangerous, explosion.' That event was not a storage-drum detonation.https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/citations/ADP004872

Notes

Closest documented historical analog to IAAAP's bulk lead azide drums. Post-1984 fate of the remaining material is a research gap.
