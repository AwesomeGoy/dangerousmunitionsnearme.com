What is documented

The same official BRAC report states that all active missions ceased and the plant closed as part of BRAC 2005 on September 30, 2009. A staff of two federal employees remain for caretaker oversight; caretaker operations are executed by Day & Zimmermann–Lone Star under a cost-plus caretaker contract. As of the November 3, 2025 update, 14,292 of 15,589 acres had been conveyed; 1,297 acres remained, with conveyance depending on cleanup. The site is on the National Priorities List. EPA's current OB/OD list still shows Lone Star Army Ammunition Plant as permitted, with a listed expiration of January 16, 2031.

Known energetics or munitions types

An official Army BRAC installation report (updated November 3, 2025; compilation updated January 23, 2026) says Lone Star Army Ammunition Plant performed a load-assemble-pack munitions production mission for more than 60 years on 11 production lines. The 2005 BRAC Commission recommended relocating storage and demilitarization functions to McAlester AAP; 105mm and 155mm ICM artillery, MLRS artillery, hand grenades, and 60mm and 81mm mortars to Milan AAP; mines and detonators/relays/delays functions to Iowa AAP; and demolition-charges functions to Crane Army Ammunition Activity. That official relocation of detonator functions to IAAAP is not, by itself, a documented origin for IAAAP's 2026 lead azide drums. Day & Zimmermann–Lone Star remains the Army's agent for two RCRA-regulated units, the High Explosives Burning Ground and the High Explosives Demolition Ground. EPA lists this EPA ID (TXR000084774) separately from Day & Zimmermann Lone Star Facility (TXR000080174).
