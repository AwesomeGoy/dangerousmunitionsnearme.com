What is documented

A former Army depot. Official Army BRAC records recommend closing Savanna Army Depot Activity and relocating the U.S. Army Defense Ammunition Center and School to McAlester Army Ammunition Plant, Oklahoma. An Army BRAC NEPA public document states that SVADA ceased operations and officially closed on May 20, 2000. The depot was listed on the EPA National Priorities List on October 15, 1984. Parcel conveyance has continued; a DENIX page updated July 15, 2026, lists starting acreage of 12,884.32, conveyed 5,363.76, and remaining 7,520.56, with current owners including USFWS, USACE, Illinois DNR, and the Jo-Carroll Depot Local Redevelopment Authority. The installation is not on EPA's current OB/OD operating list. This site still has not found an official document that accounts for the remaining bulk lead azide drums after the 1984 repack — including whether they were later destroyed, sold, or transferred (for example when the ammunition school moved to McAlester). A 1990 USADACS paper presented from Savanna discusses electrochemical reduction as a process demonstrated on lead azide, but it does not state the fate of the remaining 1984 stockpile.

Known energetics or munitions types

William Shaneyfelt, safety officer at Savanna Army Depot Activity, described Vietnam-era surplus bulk lead azide in deteriorating 55-gallon drums in a 1984 paper to the DoD Explosives Safety Board. Electrolysis destroyed about half the stockpile; the remainder was repacked. The public record reviewed for this site does not establish what happened to the remaining drums after 1984.

Notable incidents

DateVerificationWhat is documentedSources
1984verifiedShaneyfelt paper (21st Explosives Safety Seminar, Houston, Aug 28–30, 1984): surplus bulk lead azide in deteriorating drums; sale attempts failed; electrolytic destruction halted after about half the stockpile; remaining leaking drums were repacked. The paper also describes a process-building contamination accident in which sodium azide washdown reacted with brass/copper debris and caused a 'loud, though not extremely dangerous, explosion.'
