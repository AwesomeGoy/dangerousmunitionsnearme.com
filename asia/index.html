Asia

Official and primary records of 30 current and former U.S. military sites, 16 ammunition-depot explosion, landmine, unexploded ordnance (UXO) and Agent Orange exposure records, and 7 mines and tailings sites across East, Southeast, South and Central Asia, plus Diego Garcia. Of the 53 records, 38 are verified, 2 were corrected against an official or primary source, and 13 are unverified and marked as such. 9 more places are listed at the end as pointers to upcoming thematic pages.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. If you find something that may be a mine or unexploded ordnance, don’t touch it: mark the spot, move away the way you came, and tell the police or local authorities. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; Asia records are not part of alerts.

Read before using the map

  • Shaded areas are country stand-ins, not hazard footprints. Each of the 8 shaded areas is a whole country outline (GADM 4.1) standing in for reported landmine, cluster munition or UXO contamination somewhere inside it. No national authority publishes open minefield outlines.
  • Boundaries are placeholders. Country names and outlines follow GADM 4.1 and the cited sources only to place records. They are not statements on sovereignty or borders, including Kashmir, Taiwan, the South China Sea, the Korean Demilitarized Zone, the Cambodia–Thailand border and the Chagos Archipelago.
  • 13 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Yokosuka (Japan), Diego Garcia, Kandahar Airfield (Afghanistan), Phu Cat (Vietnam), Salawa depot explosion, 2016 (Sri Lanka), Ojhri Camp explosion, 1988 (Pakistan), Chbar Mon explosion, 2024 (Cambodia), Vietnam landmines and UXO, Bayan Obo (China), Marcopper (Philippines), Jaduguda (India), Kazakhstan uranium mines, Hpakant (Myanmar). They are kept for context because a key fact could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • Figures are quoted as each source gives them. Figures read only through a search summary, or from a report that could not be opened, are marked “reported”.
  • Military points are airfield-level, taken from NOAA weather-station records whose name or airport code identifies the base, not base outlines. Sasebo’s point is city-level. Philippine EDCA locations are Philippine bases with rotational U.S. access.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. 20 records have no usable official coordinate and are listed below but not mapped. Two USGS mine points that failed the boundary check (Grasberg and Jaduguda) were withheld, not corrected. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

25 points and 8 shaded country stand-ins are mapped. Unverified records have a dashed yellow outline.
