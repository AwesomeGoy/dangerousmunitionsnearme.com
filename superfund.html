What is Superfund?

Superfund is the federal program that cleans up the country’s most seriously contaminated land, such as old factories, landfills, mines and military sites. The law behind it (CERCLA, 1980) lets EPA clean up sites and makes the polluters pay for the work.

This page summarizes official public guidance. It is not medical or legal advice and it does not say whether any place is safe or unsafe. For health questions, talk to a clinician or your local health department. In an emergency, call 911 and follow local officials.

What the labels mean

  • On the National Priorities List (NPL): one of the worst sites, eligible for long-term federal cleanup. 1337 sites.
  • Proposed: EPA has proposed adding it to the list. 37 sites.
  • Deleted: EPA decided no further cleanup is needed, though some sites still have long-term monitoring or limits on land use. 466 sites.
  • Federal facility: the site is run by a federal agency, often the military or Department of Energy. 177 sites in this layer.

What you can do

  1. Read the site’s EPA profile. Every site below links to it. It lists the contaminants, cleanup progress and whether EPA considers human exposure under control.
  2. Contact the site’s EPA Community Involvement Coordinator (named on the profile) with questions, or to ask about public meetings and comment periods.
  3. Check your water. Ask your utility for its yearly water quality report, or have a private well tested by a state-certified lab.
  4. Follow posted signs and advisories about fishing, digging or entering fenced areas.
  5. Health questions: ATSDR (part of CDC) writes health assessments for many Superfund sites. Talk to your doctor or local health department.
