South America

Official and primary records of 12 current and former U.S. military sites, 5 major ammunition depot explosions, 17 landmine and unexploded ordnance (UXO) contamination records, and 35 mining operations with known hazards. Of the 69 records, 46 are verified, 13 were corrected against a newer official source, and 10 are unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. If you find something that may be a mine or unexploded ordnance, don’t touch it: mark the spot, move away the way you came, and tell the police or local authorities. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; South America records are not part of alerts.

Read before using the map

  • Landmine / UXO areas are not minefield outlines. Each shaded area is a whole department, province or territory that contains reported hazards. The only finer locations are Peru’s 39 official hazard-area points, explained below.
  • 10 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Atkinson Field (Guyana), Zanderij Field (Suriname), CAVIM depot explosions, Maracay (Venezuela), Venezuela: Apure landmine/UXO record, Candelaria copper mine (Chile), Cerro Rico de Potosí (Bolivia), Huanuni tin mine (Bolivia), Braskem rock-salt wells, Maceió (Brazil), Cerro Matoso (Colombia), Veladero gold mine (Argentina). They are kept for context because at least one key fact, usually current status, could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • Military points are airfield-level, taken from the NOAA weather station record for the airfield, not the base compound, and accurate to about 2 km.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. 15 records have no official coordinate (or, for Chile, no area left to draw) and are listed below but not mapped. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

38 points, 16 shaded areas and 39 Peru hazard-area points (at 20 distinct spots) are mapped. Unverified records have a dashed yellow outline.

Mostly World War II airfields and seaplane bases, plus later sites, from U.S. Army Center of Military History and Navy histories, the State Department, the U.S. Embassy in Quito, Navy Medicine, Ecuador’s electoral council and Colombia’s Constitutional Court. The Army and Navy histories are linked through HyperWar transcriptions because the official sites could not be reached. Central America and the Caribbean are not included.

Landmine and UXO contamination (17)

From the 2024 Article 7 reports that Colombia, Peru and Ecuador sent to the Mine Ban Treaty, Landmine Monitor 2025 and the Treaty’s Implementation Support Unit. Colombia is shown by department (12 departments, from the report’s municipal table). Shaded areas are whole departments, provinces or territories, not hazard outlines. 1 record (Chile, cleared in 2020) is list only.

Peru’s official hazard points

Peru’s treaty report (Form C and Annex I, for 2024) lists each remaining suspected hazardous area along the Ecuador border in Condorcanqui province, Amazonas, with a coordinate. The map shows 39 of the 46 as small dark-red diamonds (Cenepa 20, Achuime 18, Santiago 1), converted from the printed UTM zone 17S coordinates. Each marks one suspected area as Peru printed it; it is not an outline, and the areas are small (together 202,526 m²). All 18 Achuime areas share one printed coordinate, so they appear as one marker. Seven were left off: two rows have misprinted coordinates, one converts to a point inside Ecuador, and four sit on the Cordillera del Cóndor border where boundary datasets disagree. Peru’s own totals also don’t match: the area list adds up to 271,206 m², but its summary says 279,388 m² (the summary is shown).
