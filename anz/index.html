Australia, New Zealand and the Pacific

Official Australian and New Zealand records of areas affected by unexploded ordnance (UXO), military PFAS sites, ammunition sea-dumping areas and other munitions records, plus links to contaminated-land registers. Every record is from a government source and is labeled partly verified. Blank means no official value was found.

Also on this page: 9 records from the wider Pacific and Australian and New Zealand mine sites

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. In Australia, if you find something that may be UXO, don’t touch it; call 000. In New Zealand, call 111. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; Australia and New Zealand records are not part of alerts.

Australian UXO points are the official map’s centre points, not the area outlines. PFAS points are base locations, not where contamination was found.

    Long-term monitoring (rounds Oct 2020 to early 2025) "shows very little long-term change" (NZDF evidence, Apr 2025). Nov 2023 round: 7 bores above drinking-water standard, concentrations within historical ranges (per RNZ report of the monitoring report). Ōhakea Rural Water Scheme opened 2022, serving the base and 90+ households/farms, 7 of them PFAS-affected. Horizons reviewed PFAS monitoring near Makowhai Stream in 2025-26.

Oct/Nov 2024 Crown paid Kurahaupō iwi $25.2m because contamination stopped purchase of remaining base land.

Ammunition explosions (2)

  • West Loch disaster (LST ammunition explosions), Pearl Harbor — Hawaii, United States Verified
    Explosion · 1944-05-21
    Deaths: 163 · Injured: 396
    NHHC: on 21 May 1944 LST-353, at berth T-8 in West Loch with 29 combat-loaded LSTs staging for Saipan, exploded; further explosions and fires followed into 22 May; 6 LSTs, 3 LCTs, 17 amphibious tractors and 8 howitzers lost; burning LSTs stopped about 500 ft from the ammunition depot on the south shore. Cause never established; lessons: separate gasoline and ammunition loading, enforce smoking bans, redesign LST berths.
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
    Related on this site: Navy Region Hawaii – Waipio Peninsula (site page).
    Sources: Naval History and Heritage Command, 'Pearl Harbor Ablaze Again: The West Loch Disaster' (A. Bisno, May 2019)

  • Ranger uranium mine (Alligator Rivers Region) — Northern Territory, Australia Verified
    Former open-cut uranium mine, under rehabilitation · Operator: Energy Resources of Australia (ERA) · 1981-2021
    DCCEEW: 8 km east of Jabiru, within the 78 km2 Ranger Project Area, surrounded by but separate from World Heritage Kakadu National Park, next to Magela Creek (East Alligator River); produced U3O8 by acid leach from 1981; mining ceased 2012 and stockpiled ore was processed until 2021; rehabilitation under way; Supervising Scientist monitoring has shown people and environment around Ranger remained protected.

  • Rum Jungle former uranium mine — Northern Territory, Australia Verified
    Former uranium and copper mine; acid and metalliferous drainage · Operator: Australian Government (historic); NT Government Rum Jungle Rehabilitation team · 1953-1971 (NT history page)
    NT Government: mine operated 1953-1971, producing 3,530 t uranium oxide and 20,000 t copper concentrate; legacy landforms, groundwater and surface-water contamination and AMD impacts on the East Branch of the Finniss River; Australian Government rehabilitation 1983-86 ($18.6 million) later deteriorated; joint rehabilitation since 2009 with the Kungarakan and Warai people; Stage 3 began early 2022 and is expected to span 15 years (pit backfill with lime-amended rock, new covered waste storage, river realignment).

  • Mount Lyell copper mine acid drainage (Queen and King rivers, Macquarie Harbour) — Tasmania, Australia Verified
    Historic copper-gold mine; legacy AMD, tailings and slag · Operator: Copper Mines of Tasmania (current operator, not responsible for legacy AMD)
    EPA Tasmania: copper and gold mined for more than a century; smelter slag and tailings discharged to the rivers until smelting stopped in 1970, and tailings discharge ceased in 1994; legacy AMD from tunnels and waste-rock dumps into the Queen and King rivers is by far the most significant ongoing harm; Mt Lyell Acid Drainage Remediation Act 2003; 2002 investigations found no cost-neutral treatment option; 2013 request for proposals produced none meeting the Act's criteria.
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.

  • Pike River coal mine explosion — West Coast, New Zealand Verified
    Underground coal mine disaster (methane explosion) · Operator: Pike River Coal Ltd · 2010-11-19
    Deaths: 29
    Royal Commission: at 3:45 pm on 19 Nov 2010 the mine exploded; 29 men underground died; two men in the 2.3 km stone drift escaped; three more explosions over nine days before the mine was sealed; immediate cause was ignition of a substantial volume of methane; in the last 48 days 21 reports of explosive methane levels and 27 of lesser dangerous levels; health and safety systems inadequate; the Department of Labour lacked focus and capacity to ensure compliance.
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.

  • Panguna mine (Bougainville) and Kawerong-Jaba river tailings — Bougainville, Papua New Guinea Verified
    Former open-pit copper-gold mine; riverine tailings · Operator: Bougainville Copper Ltd (BCL), 'majority-owned by Rio Tinto' per Rio Tinto's 2024 release · 1972-1989
    ABG (27 Apr 2023): Panguna Mine Legacy Impact Assessment field work covering impacts since mining stopped in 1989 - Loloho port, the mine, the Kawerong and Jaba rivers, Kuneka Creek, tailings-deposition areas and the delta at Empress Augusta Bay.
