What is FUSRAP?

FUSRAP, the Formerly Utilized Sites Remedial Action Program, cleans up places contaminated by the early U.S. atomic energy and nuclear weapons program in the 1940s to 1960s, such as old uranium and thorium processing plants, warehouses and the land around them. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) does the cleanup. When it is finished, the Department of Energy’s Office of Legacy Management (DOE LM) takes over long-term care.

This page summarizes official public guidance. It is not medical or legal advice and it does not say whether any place is safe or unsafe. For health questions, talk to a clinician or your local health department. In an emergency, call 911 and follow local officials.

The 20 sites USACE is still actively working on, as listed by DOE LM and USACE. This layer was checked against USACE, DOE LM and EPA records on Nov 25, 2026. 17 have a checked map location. 3 (Joslyn Manufacturing and Supply Company, DuPont Chambers Works, Staten Island Warehouse) are marked “location not confirmed,” so they are listed below but are not on the map and are not used for ZIP distances.

Common contaminants are uranium, thorium and radium left in soil, buildings or groundwater. Being near a site does not by itself mean anyone is exposed. Most contamination is in soil or buildings on the site.

What you can do

  1. Read the USACE page for the site (linked below). It explains what was found, what cleanup is planned and whom to contact.
  2. Contact the USACE district named for the site with questions, or to ask about public meetings and comment periods.
  3. Follow posted signs and don’t enter fenced or restricted areas or dig on or next to a site.
  4. Health questions: talk to your doctor or local health department. ATSDR (part of CDC) has written health assessments for some FUSRAP sites.
