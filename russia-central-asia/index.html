Russia and Central Asia

Official and primary records of 12 ammunition-depot explosions, 4 military and test legacy sites, and 18 mine disasters and uranium and tailings legacy sites in Russia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Uzbekistan, with a section for Belarus, Moldova, Armenia, Azerbaijan and Georgia. Of the 34 records, 23 are verified, 2 were corrected against an official or primary source, and 9 are unverified and marked as such. 33 places already recorded elsewhere on this site are listed at the end as pointers.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. If you find something that may be a mine or unexploded ordnance, don’t touch it: mark the spot, move away the way you came, and tell the police or local authorities. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; these records are not part of alerts.

Read before using this page

  • Boundaries are placeholders. Country and region names follow GADM 4.1 and the cited sources only to place records. They are not statements on sovereignty or borders, including Crimea, Transnistria, Abkhazia, South Ossetia and Nagorno-Karabakh.
  • State media are labeled. Where a record rests on Russian state media (TASS, RIA Novosti or regional government newspapers) or Turkish state media, the source is marked “state media” and figures that no official text confirms are marked reported.
  • 9 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Toropets explosions after drone attack (Tver Oblast), Kamenny / Tikhoretsk district detonations after drone attack (Krasnodar Krai), Ministry of Defence warehouse explosion, Malik settlement (Syrdarya region), Raspadskaya coal mine explosions (Kuzbass), Ulyanovskaya coal mine explosion (Kuzbass), State Border Service military unit ammunition store explosion and fire, Gobustan settlement (Garadagh, Baku), Explosive-remnant contamination around former Soviet military bases, Georgia, Chiatura manganese mining (Georgian Manganese) and Kvirila River pollution, Soligorsk potash salt tailings piles and clay-salt sludge storages (Belaruskali). Figures in them are reported, not confirmed in an official source.
  • Figures are quoted as each source gives them. Where official sources disagree (for example injury counts for the 2009 Ulyanovsk arsenal fire), the record says so.
  • Only 2 records are mapped, at USGS facility points; 32 have no official coordinate and are list only. Nothing was geocoded.

Military and test legacy (2)

  • Vozrozhdeniya Island Soviet biological-weapons test site (Aral Sea) — Uzbekistan Verified
    Former BW open-air test site and anthrax burial pits
    The DoD point paper (Jan 2002) describes 11 side-by-side unlined burial pits, each about 4 ft wide, 10 ft long and 4-6 ft deep, reportedly containing weaponized anthrax believed to come from the BW facility at Yekaterinburg (formerly Sverdlovsk). In Oct 1997 a US team took 64 pit samples, 36 from laboratory buildings and 110 from around the test facility; about 11 of the 64 pit samples tested positive for live anthrax spores, and one building sample for plague. The CTR plan aimed to decontaminate the pits and demolish all buildings.
    Status: former; burial pits targeted by US CTR decontamination plan (2001-2002)

  • Baikonur Proton-M launch failure and heptyl contamination — Kazakhstan Verified
    Rocket crash on the cosmodrome (Russian-leased territory)
    A Proton-M with a DM-03 upper stage and three Glonass-M satellites, launched on 2 Jul 2013, fell and exploded about ten seconds after launch between pads 81 and 200 on territory leased by Russia. Kazakhstan's Environment Minister said that heptyl (UDMH) above the permitted concentration was not found in air or soil in the surrounding villages. Kazcosmos said in Sep 2013 that detoxification was complete: 'heptyl content in the soil was reduced from 8850 MPC to the level of 952. Now there is a normal level of heptyl - about 1 MPC'.
    Status: soil detoxification declared complete (Sep 2013)
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
