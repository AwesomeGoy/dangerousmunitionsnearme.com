About this data. This Europe map is compiled from official EMODnet dumped-munitions records. It isn’t the only source — always check the original source and follow its safety guidance.

Europe

A map of 9,737 official EMODnet dumped-munitions records in European and adjacent waters, including 8,322 OSPAR encounter reports. Pins are clustered; color marks munition type. Country is derived from Marine Regions exclusive economic zones, not from EMODnet itself.

Most of these points are encounters, not official dump sites

This section plots 9,737 official EMODnet Human Activities dumped-munitions records: 9,459 points and 278 areas (shown as centroids, with official outlines on 255 of them). 8,322 of the 9,459 point records cite OSPAR ODIMS as the information provider. Those are munitions found by fishermen, surveys, or other sea users — encounter reports that EMODnet aggregated into its dumped-munitions layer — not independently confirmed designated dump sites. See OSPAR’s dumped munitions overview and OSPAR Recommendation 2010/20 (as amended) on reporting encounters.

These objects can still be dangerous. Sea-dumped munitions remain a hazard to fishermen, divers, and people on beaches, even after many decades. The UK Maritime and Coastguard Agency’s MGN 323 (M+F) Amendment 1, Explosives picked up at sea, says suspected explosives should not be deliberately landed on deck, and that if they are taken on board they must not be cleaned, opened, or tampered with; the Coastguard should be informed immediately by radio. If you find a suspicious object on a beach, do not touch or move it; keep people away and contact local emergency services or the Coastguard.

Scotland’s own NMPi historic munitions layer is not in this cut. The Scottish Government Marine Directorate National Marine Plan interactive historic munitions disposal sites layer (layer information) is not downloadable: it is not offered as a download, and the published WFS requires a login, so those official Scottish points and the charted disposal polygon were not ingested. Some EMODnet records do fall in Scottish Marine Regions and are labelled when that region name is known.
