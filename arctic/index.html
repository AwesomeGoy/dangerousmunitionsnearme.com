Arctic

Official and primary records of 13 military bases, radar sites and programmes, 14 nuclear test sites, weapon accidents, sunken submarines and dumped reactors, 4 contamination, waste and explosion records, and 14 mines, smelters and industrial spills across the Arctic and the sub-Arctic edge (Alaska, Canada, Greenland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia). Of the 45 records, 25 are verified, 4 were corrected against a better official source, and 16 are unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; Arctic records are not part of alerts.

Read before using the map

  • 16 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Evenes Air Station, Nagurskoye airfield, Temp airfield (Kotelny Island), Severomorsk, Tiksi airfield, Novaya Zemlya test site, Andreeva Bay, Nyonoksa 2019 explosion, Severomorsk depot explosions (1984), Norilsk-Talnakh district, Monchegorsk smelter complex, Kvanefjeld project, Raglan mine, Kittilä mine, Nanisivik mine, Polaris mine. They are kept for context because a key fact could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • Russian military sites are not confirmed. No Russian Ministry of Defence source was reached for Nagurskoye, Temp (Kotelny Island), Severomorsk or Tiksi. Their map points are NOAA weather stations at those places, not base outlines, and the military roles described for them come from secondary reporting and are not confirmed.
  • Thule 1968 B-52 crash: mapped at the impact point printed in the 1969 Strategic Air Command study of the clean-up (Project Crested Ice). Other official accounts give a slightly different distance and direction from the runway.
  • The DEW Line is one record. It is a single programme record (plus one clean-up record) with no station points, because the official source lists no station coordinates.
  • Amchitka is mapped at island level. The point is the island’s weather station, not the ground zeros of the 1965, 1969 and 1971 tests.
  • Three points are flagged as just outside boundaries: Temp on Kotelny Island (0 m) and Severomorsk (190 m) sit just outside the generalized coastline in the boundary data, and the K-27 submarine sits 68 m inside the land outline because the data fills in its narrow fjord. Each was checked and kept, and each record says so.
  • Some sites are just south of the Arctic Circle. The Aleutian sites (Shemya, Amchitka), interior Alaska (Eielson, Clear), Keflavik, Raglan and Nyonoksa are included as Arctic-adjacent.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. 14 records have no usable official coordinate and are listed below but not mapped. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

31 records are mapped. Map view: a north polar stereographic projection (EPSG:3413), looking down on the North Pole, so far-northern places are not stretched the way they are on ordinary web maps. Greenland is at the centre bottom and 45°W points down. The land outline is Natural Earth 1:50m, simplified to about 5 km; the red dashed line is the Arctic Circle. There are no street-map tiles. Camp Century has three records at one point (the base, its PM-2A reactor and its wastes) and Amchitka has two, so zoom in or use the list.

Nuclear tests, accidents and dumped reactors (14; 6 mapped)

The 1968 Thule nuclear-weapon accident, the PM-2A reactor at Camp Century, the Novaya Zemlya and Amchitka test sites, Soviet reactors and submarines dumped or sunk in the Kara and Barents seas, Andreeva Bay, Project Chariot, Russian lighthouse RTGs and the 2019 Nyonoksa explosion. Figures are from the Norwegian Radiation and Nuclear Safety Authority (DSA), the IAEA, the U.S. Department of Energy and the CTBTO.

  • Thule B-52 crash with four nuclear weapons (Project Crested Ice), Wolstenholme Fjord — United States Corrected
    Nuclear-weapon accident (Broken Arrow); plutonium dispersal on sea ice · 1968-01-21
    4 nuclear weapons destroyed by fire (conventional HE detonated, no nuclear yield); 6 of 7 crew survived; ~237,000 cu ft of contaminated ice, snow, water and debris removed
    Contaminated ice, snow and debris removed to the U.S. in a four-month operation; residual contamination not quantified here
    Mapped at the impact point printed in the 1969 SAC Historical Study 113 (Project Crested Ice), on the ice of Wolstenholme Fjord, 44,500 feet (about 8.4 miles) almost due west of the runway end. Other official accounts say about 7 to 7½ miles southwest; the SAC point is used. Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: USAF Strategic Air Command, 'Project Crested Ice: The Thule Nuclear Accident', SAC Historical Study No. 113, v · DoD, 'Narrative Summaries of Accidents Involving U.S. Nuclear Weapons 1950-1980' (Thule entry; Internet Archiv · FRUS 1964-68 vol.
