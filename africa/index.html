Africa

Official and primary records of 19 current and former U.S. military sites, 7 major ammunition depot explosions, landmine and unexploded ordnance (UXO) contamination in 22 countries and territories, and 30 mining operations with known hazards. Of the 78 records, 50 are verified, 22 were corrected against a newer official source, and 6 are unverified and marked as such.

South Africa in depth: the South Africa page adds 51 records on military bases and ranges, the former nuclear weapons programme, reactors and waste, munitions explosions and mining hazards.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. If you find something that may be a mine or unexploded ordnance, don’t touch it: mark the spot, move away the way you came, and tell the police or local authorities. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; Africa records are not part of alerts.

Read before using the map

  • Landmine / UXO areas are not minefield outlines. Each shaded area is a whole province or country that contains reported hazards. No national authority publishes open hazard outlines.
  • 6 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: N'Djamena cooperative security location (Chad), NAS Port Lyautey / Kenitra (Morocco), Egypt landmine/UXO record, Williamson diamond mine (Tanzania), Sino-Metals Leach Zambia (Chambishi), Ruashi copper-cobalt mine (DR Congo). They are kept for context because at least one key fact, usually current status, could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • Military points are airfield-level, taken from the official airfield reference point or NOAA weather station, not the base compound. Some are only accurate to about 2 to 5 km.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. 20 records have no official coordinate and are listed below but not mapped. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

36 points and 22 shaded areas are mapped. Unverified records have a dashed yellow outline.

Ammunition depot explosions (7; 2 mapped)

Major events from IFRC, OCHA, UNOSAT and government records. This is not a complete list; the Small Arms Survey database of such events is available by request only. Two blast epicentres were read from official UNOSAT maps; the rest have no official coordinate.

  • Ikeja Military Cantonment ammunition depot explosions, Lagos — Nigeria, Lagos Verified
    2002-01-27. Deaths: over 1,000 (government officials, as reported by IFRC). Injured: many (no official total located). Displaced: about 12,000; 535 missing (IFRC).
    Explosions at the army ammunition depot sent shells across Lagos; many deaths were people who drowned in a canal while fleeing (IFRC). UXO remained outside the cantonment.
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: IFRC, Nigeria: Munitions Depot Explosion in Lagos, Appeal 03/2002, Operations Update no. 2 · IFRC Emergency Appeal 03/2002 (31 Jan 2002)
  • Malhazine arms depot explosions, Maputo — Mozambique, Maputo City Verified
    2007-03-22. Deaths: 101 (IFRC Information Bulletin no. 2: 19 in hospitals, 82 in residential areas). Injured: about 492. Displaced: 64 houses destroyed (CENOE).
    Explosions lasted about four hours and launched rockets into nearby neighbourhoods. IFRC notes earlier depot explosions in September 1985 and January 2007.
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: IFRC, Mozambique: Munitions Explosion, Information Bulletin no. 2 (30 Mar 2007)
