South Africa

A closer look at South Africa: 13 military bases, test ranges and munitions plants, 8 records on the former nuclear weapons programme, reactors and radioactive waste, 6 explosions, training accidents and unexploded-ordnance records, and 24 mining, tailings and mine-disaster records. Of the 51 records, 38 are verified, 3 were corrected against a newer official source, and 10 are unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. ZIP alerts on this site cover U.S. sites only; South African records are not part of alerts.

Read before using the map

  • 10 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: AFB Waterkloof, AFB Makhado, AFB Hoedspruit, AFB Langebaanweg, AFB Swartkop, Vela “double flash”, 1979, Lohatla fire, 2023, Naschem explosion, 1990, Middelbult explosion, 1993, UXO on military ranges. They are kept for context because a key fact could not be confirmed in an official source.
  • Air Force bases are not confirmed in an official source. The Air Force and Defence websites could not be reached, so the squadrons listed come from a non-official site. Four bases are mapped at the NOAA weather station on the airfield; Swartkop is list only because its NOAA point is about 100 km off.
  • The 1979 Vela “double flash” is officially unresolved. U.S. government bodies disagreed about whether it was a nuclear test, and nothing has been officially confirmed.
  • Other unverified records: the 2023 Lohatla fire (only a parliamentary statement based on media reports), the 1990 Naschem explosion (local press only), the 1993 Middelbult explosion (industry body only) and unexploded ordnance on military ranges (no official inventory exists publicly).
  • The Pelindaba point is a site-area sample, published by Necsa for the site, not the SAFARI-1 reactor building.
  • 18 mine records come from USGS 2018 data. Their commodity, operator and status values are as USGS listed them in 2018 and current operators were not re-checked, except where noted (Mponeng).
  • Jagersfontein and Marikana are already on the Africa page, so they are linked, not mapped twice.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. 24 records have no usable official coordinate and are listed below but not mapped, including all 6 explosion and accident records. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

27 records are mapped. Several West Rand gold mines (Mponeng, Kusasalethu, Driefontein, Kloof, South Deep) are within a few kilometres of each other, so zoom in.

Nuclear weapons legacy, reactors and waste (8; 3 mapped)

South Africa built six nuclear devices in the 1980s and dismantled them before joining the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty in 1991, the only country to give up nuclear weapons it built itself. This section covers the former weapons sites the IAEA verified, the Pelindaba enrichment plant and SAFARI-1 reactor, the Koeberg nuclear power station, the Vaalputs waste site and the unresolved 1979 Vela event.

  • ARMSCOR 'Circle' facilities (Kentron Circle), former nuclear weapons production and storage — Gauteng (near Pretoria) Verified
    Former nuclear weapons facility · built 1980; commissioned May 1981; weapons work ended Nov 1989
    Operator: Armscor (from 1979)
    Decommissioned; uranium metallurgy area dismantled and decontaminated; to be used for non-nuclear commercial purposes
    Six gun-assembled devices completed (first qualified production model Aug 1987) plus HEU core and some components of a seventh; all HEU returned to the AEC 14 Mar-6 Sep 1991; weapons produced and stored in secured vaults
    List only: no official coordinate was found, so it is not on the map (not geocoded). Checked 2026-11-25.
    Sources: IAEA Bulletin 37/1 (1995), von Baeckmann, Dillon & Perricos, 'Nuclear verification in South Africa' · President F.W. de Klerk, speech to Parliament on the dismantling of South Africa's nuclear weapons, 24 Mar 199

    Research reactor (isotope production) · first criticality Mar 1965

    Operating
