Military munitions dumped at sea

For decades the U.S. military got rid of unwanted bombs, shells, bullets and chemical weapons by dumping them in the ocean. The Department of Defense says this happened in U.S. waters between 1917 and 1970, and that it has not disposed of munitions at sea since 1970.

This page summarizes official public guidance. It is not medical or legal advice and it does not say whether any place is safe or unsafe. For health questions, talk to a clinician or your local health department. In an emergency, call 911 and follow local officials.

What this list is

DoD’s last required inventory, in its FY2009 Defense Environmental Restoration Program report to Congress, lists 72 sea-disposal sites in U.S. waters. 23 of them include chemical weapons covered by the Chemical Weapons Convention; the rest held conventional munitions or other materials. For each site DoD published the distance from shore (in nautical miles), the water depth (in feet) and a table of what was dumped. Every entry here was checked against those tables on Nov 25, 2026.

Why most sites are not on the map

DoD’s report does not give coordinates. Only one site, HI-06 Ordnance Reef off Wai‘anae, O‘ahu, can be tied to an official charted location: the “Unexploded Ordnance” area on NOAA chart 19357. Its map point is approximate (the middle of that charted area). The other 71 sites are listed with their distance from shore and depth only. Some nautical charts show general “dumping areas”, but they are not labeled with these site codes, so they are not plotted here.

What to do if you find something

Old munitions can wash up on beaches or come up in fishing gear and dredges. Follow DoD’s 3Rs of explosives safety: Recognize that it may be a munition, Retreat without touching or moving it, and Report it by calling 911.
