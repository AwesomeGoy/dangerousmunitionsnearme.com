Nuclear test sites

Where nuclear weapons were tested, by testing state: test counts, largest yields, contamination and compensation programs, from official sources. 35 records: 23 verified, 0 corrected against a better official source and 12 unverified and marked as such.

This page is not an official government document and does not say any place is safe or unsafe. It is a selection of documented places, not a complete list. ZIP alerts on this site do not include these records.

Read before using this page

  • 12 records are unverified and labeled in yellow: Johnston Atoll (Hardtack I high-altitude shots, Dominic/Fishbowl), Semipalatinsk Test Site (Polygon), Kazakhstan, Chagan / Lake Balapan ('Atomic Lake'), Semipalatinsk, Totskoye range (Snowball exercise), Orenburg Oblast, USSR peaceful nuclear explosion programme (outside test sites), Christmas Island (Kiritimati) — UK Grapple X, Y and Z, Malden Island — UK Grapple (1957), Reggane (CSEM), Algerian Sahara, In Ekker (CEMO, Taourirt Tan Afella), Algerian Sahara, Ras Koh, Chagai Hills, Balochistan, Kharan Desert, Balochistan, Punggye-ri nuclear test site, North Hamgyong. Figures in them are reported, not confirmed in an official source.
  • Test counts are each testing state’s official count as the cited source gives it. Where official sources disagree (for example UK MOD and Australian ARPANSA yields, or India’s and Pakistan’s announced device counts against the UN’s count of tests), both are shown.
  • Mapped points are test ground zeros or IAEA-printed points, not site boundaries. Six official coordinates (Bikini, Enewetak, Runit Dome, Christmas Island U.S. tests, Moruroa and Fangataufa) failed the land-boundary check or contain a known error, so those records are list only.
  • Blank coordinates mean list only. Nothing was geocoded or guessed.

12 records are mapped as points. Unverified records have a dashed yellow outline. The other 23 records have no usable official coordinate and are listed below only.
